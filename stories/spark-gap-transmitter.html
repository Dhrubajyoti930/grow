Spark-Gap Transmitter

In the late 19th century, physicists weren't looking for music or news. They were playing with lightning.

By discharging high-voltage capacitors across a narrow gap, they created violent, brilliant arcs of electricity. It was crude, it was dangerous, and it was loud.

But those sparks were doing something unexpected. Every pop of electricity sent a ripple through the unseen fabric of the ether—electromagnetic waves.
