Why The Sky Is Purple

[ PHOTON SCATTERING SIM ]

High in the thermosphere, the sun sends a chaotic burst of all colors—the full spectrum of light. As these waves strike our atmosphere, they collide with gas molecules, scattering like billiard balls.

Violet and blue light have the shortest, most energetic wavelengths. They scatter the most, flooding the sky in a violet hue. So, why do we see blue?

It is not the sky that changes; it is the eye. Human eyes are significantly more sensitive to blue light than violet. Furthermore, the sun emits less violet light to begin with. Our brain takes this violet-drenched scatter, suppresses the deep indigo, and resolves it into the familiar, gentle blue of our world.
