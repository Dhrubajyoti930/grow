Slower Than Light

We imagine the flip of a switch as an instantaneous event. A snap of fingers, a flood of light. We treat electricity like a ghost—omnipresent and immediate.

But inside the copper veins of your walls, the reality is much more sluggish. Electrons do not sprint at the speed of light; they drift. They are dancers in a crowded ballroom, bumping into atoms, losing energy, creeping forward at a mere fraction of a millimeter per second.

If you were to watch a single electron inside your lamp's wire, it would take hours to travel the length of a single meter. The power you feel is not the movement of matter, but the pulse of a field—the message arriving long before the messenger.

Drift Velocity: ~0.0001 m/s
