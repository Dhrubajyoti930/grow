Weighing The Earth

In 1798, Henry Cavendish stepped into a quiet shed in Clapham. He didn't have a giant scale. He had a torsion balance—a delicate, spindly contraption designed to detect the faint pull of gravity between two lead spheres.

The Earth pulls everything toward its core. By measuring how much these small lead weights twisted a wire toward larger ones, Cavendish calculated the "Gravitational Constant." It was the first time a human weighed the entire planet.
